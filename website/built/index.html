What is built

The reality file for this repository. If it is not listed here as built, it does not exist. The plan describes intentions; this page describes facts, and it is updated in the same commit as the code it describes.

Status words, as the VoiceDebrief family uses them: built is in the repository and tested; unverified is written but has not yet been proven by a run; proposed does not exist.

Push and pull unverifiedPackages/SGitVault/Sources/SGitVault/VaultSync.swift, VaultBook.push, VaultBook.pull: push sends the objects the server's head cannot reach, each checked against its id, in batches of at most 50 objects and 3 MB, then moves the server's current with compare-and-swap (onto a server that does not hold the vault: the ref and the branch index, as creation does). Pull is the same the other way. Only creation and fast-forward: when the other side is ahead, or both have changed, it says so and writes nothing. Each vault shows one line: on this device only, not yet pushed, on this device and on the server (when), ahead of the server, or push failed with the reasonUnit tests with a folder in the server's place, in the engine and in the app: first push, a later push sending only the new objects, pull into an empty device, pull forward, a device that is behind, both sides changed (neither ref moves, nothing copied), a tampered object refused. ios-vault.yml has sgit clone the vault that was created in one folder and pushed to another, and verify its signatures. Vault keys and server tokens in iCloud Keychain unverifiedEngine/SecretStore.swift (SecretHome, SharedSecrets), VaultBook, the Keys section of UI/VaultsView.swift: each vault's key, each server and each server's token is a Keychain item of its own, kept on this device and, unless the switch is turned off, as a synchronisable item as well, so that iCloud Keychain can carry it to the owner's other devices. On by default. A key that arrives shows its vault as on another device, or on its server, until a copy is kept here. Forgetting a vault on one device removes its key from iCloud Keychain; a device that holds the vault keeps its own copy and does not put the key back. Turning the switch off takes everything onto this device, then removes it from iCloud Keychain. The key that signs a device's commits is never synchronised. What earlier builds kept in one list becomes an item eachUnit tests on the CI simulator with an in-memory Keychain in which two stores share their synchronised items: on by default and the signing key never shared; a vault made on one device appearing on a second, copied there and written from both; two devices adding vaults at once; forgetting on one device; the switch off and on; one device turning it off while another keeps its keys; the earlier lists adopted. Nothing in CI can test iCloud Keychain itself: whether Apple carries these items between two real devices is unconfirmed until the owner sees a vault made on his iPhone appear on his iPad or Mac

Known limits of what is built

  • Live vault writes from Swift work; sgit has not yet opened an app-made vault from the live server. On 13 November 2026 the app created a vault on the live server and saved a debrief into it (a commit to an existing branch, with compare-and-swap), reading both back with signatures verified. In CI sgit clones a Swift-created vault from a folder. Cloning the live one with sgit on a computer is the remaining check.
  • An iPad works, and reports a larger model. A note recorded on an iPad on 13 November 2026 (build 0.1.7) was transcribed and debriefed; the model there reported a context of 8,192 tokens, against 4,096 on the iPhone and the Mac.
  • Build 0.1.9 on a device (13 November 2026). Three notes of 89, 95 and 169 seconds were recorded and sent by the owner from build 0.1.9 (44), with a 4,096-token model. The live words now stay in view; the green control and the Discard at the top were used and liked. Found: the Send button moved as the note filled in and could not be pressed until the end (issue 026). The clean text, the recipients screen and the QR scanner have not been reported on.
  • A long note works. 6 minutes 44 seconds on an iPad (build 0.1.7): 65 lines transcribed in 5.4 seconds; the whole transcript fitted the iPad's 8,192-token model in one request and the debrief took 10.5 seconds, with six fair key points. The audio was 1.7 MB and crossed the lane intact in one part. Reading in parts has still never run on a device.
  • The debrief loses the thread on a rambling note. Of the two longer notes, one debrief was fair and the other garbled a spoken aside into an action ("refresh app whenever movement, sub, sub, sub, or focus gain occurs") and gave a task to "[Claude]" because the note addressed Claude by name. The transcripts were good.
  • Installed, not yet judged. Build 0.1.0 (4) was installed from TestFlight on an iPhone and on an Apple silicon Mac and opened on both. The transcript and debrief arrive in the next build, and whether Apple's models are good enough on real voice notes is the open question of the whole project.
  • The model's language may not match the note's. The debrief is asked for in the language that was transcribed. Whether Apple's model writes European Portuguese well, or at all, is not known.
  • The identity has not been on a device. The formats are proven against sgit and pki.sgit.ai's code in CI. iCloud Keychain carrying the user's key to a second device, the Secure Enclave key, writing the vouching statement into a vault, and the round trip with pki.sgit.ai are all untried. Two devices making an identity before iCloud Keychain has caught up would make two: the screen says to wait and look again, and nothing enforces it.
  • No private key is committed, not even a throwaway one. The unit tests' fixtures hold public material only, so opening sgit's passphrase-protected keys and its envelopes is checked in pki-interop.yml alone, on keys made in the run.
  • The four spikes in the plan have not been run: passkey bytes in a native app, the quality of Apple's models on real voice notes, live vault writes, and Ogg Opus decoding. Each can change the plan.
  • Push and pull have only met folders. Every test of them puts a folder where the server would be. The requests are the ones creation and saving already make to the live server, but no vault has yet been pushed from the app to dev.send.sgraph.ai, or pulled from it.
  • A vault's data is only as safe as the device until it is pushed. Its key now travels through iCloud Keychain (unverified, above), but the vault itself exists only on the device that made it until it is pushed to a server. A key that reaches a second device before the vault has been pushed opens nothing there, and the app says so. With the switch off, the key is in the Keychain of this device only and in no backup.
  • A vault on the device is stored twice. The reader keeps its own cache of objects beside the vault's folder. Wasteful, not wrong.

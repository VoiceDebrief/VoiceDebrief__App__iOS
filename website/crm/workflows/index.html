SGit CRM as workflows made of cells

How SGit CRM is put together, and what it sells: cells, combined into workflows, combined into plays, which together are the CRM. Written 16 November 2026 by the CRM cell (cells/crm-ios/) at the owner's request, after the ten workflows mapped for Algarve Evolution (Ten agentic cells for the death by a thousand cuts, a prototype not yet approved by Algarve Evolution). Status: six workflows are built (W1, W3, W5, W11, W12, W13) and the rest are proposed; each says which.

The idea in four layers

LayerWhat it isWhat a buyer getsIn the app
CellOne unit of work, with its own reach: a piece of code, one model call, an agent's loop, or a person's decisionA part that can be tested alone and reused in other workflowsA Swift type or a lane, with a test
WorkflowCells joined end to end, from a trigger to a record, with a policy and a person's approval before anything leavesOne job done the way a person would do it, only faster: "import the summit contacts", "draft a follow-up"A screen that runs it, and a commit at the end
PlayA named sequence of workflows the team repeats ("after a conference", "an investor round")A way of workingA plays/ record in the vault (the format has them)
The CRMThe plays, the workflows they use, and the records they leave in the user's vaultThe product on the App StoreSGit CRM

So we do not sell "a CRM" first. We sell workflows, each made of cells we have tested, and the CRM is what you have once you run several of them. A customer can start with one workflow; the AgentiCCells ladder (one agent, a few sessions, a team, a network) says how far it grows.

Kinds of cell, by altitude

The altitudes of a run (from the Swarm demo of 12 November, in six setups of an agentic system) are the kinds of cell. Each kind has a cost, a reach and a place it may run.

AltitudeCellExample in the CRMCostWhere it runs
A0Code cell: plain code, deterministic, testedthe vCard reader, the matcher, the commitnothing per runin the app
A1On-device model cell: one call to Apple's models (Vision, the Foundation Models framework)reading the text on a badge photonothing per run; nothing leaves the devicein the app
A1Cloud model cell: one call to Claude, or through OpenRouterdrafting a follow-up in our voiceper calloutside the app, behind a lane: the app holds no API key
A2, A3Agent cell: a loop with tools and a key of its ownmerging duplicates, enriching with sourcesper runa Claude session, writing proposals to an append lane
PPerson cell: a decision only a person may takeaccepting a proposal, sending a messagea person's timein the app

Cells move down the altitudes as we learn. A job is first done by an agent, because nobody knows its shape yet; once its shape is known, an agent writes it as code, and the code cell replaces the agent cell: fewer moving parts, no cost per run, nothing to reach. The card-text reader is already one: plain rules where a model might have been, tested on invented cards. Every cell keeps the same promise wherever it sits: the policy says what it can reach (the grant), what it was asked to do (the mandate), the difference (the gap) and what stops it (the barriers).

What every workflow has

As in the Algarve Evolution map: a trigger (a vault, a photo, a voice note, a date, a reply); cells that read, match and draft; a policy check; a person who approves anything that leaves or changes a record; a record of what changed and which key changed it (one signed commit per batch, in the user's CRM vault). No workflow sends a message: the CRM drafts, a person sends.

Plays that use them

  • After a conference (the Web Summit play): W1 import every team source, W8 merge, W3 the list, W4 a draft for each, W5 log, W9 the weekly view, until every follow-up is done or parked.
  • At a stand: W2 for each person met, W6 for each "you should talk to", W7 at the end of the day.
  • Our own team: W10 every hour, W3 for what each agent is waiting on.

What the app does today

W1 is built: the cells are Sources/Import/ (readers, the team CRM layout, vaults of vaults, the matcher, the planner), CardPhotoReader (Vision), Engine/CRMVault.swift (the signed commit), and the review screen (the person cell). Each cell has its own unit tests on invented people. What the next steps of the brief build fall into W3 (step 4), W4 and W5 (step 5), W8 and W10 (steps 6 and 7). The app's Workflows section lists the thirteen with their cells and whether each is built (Model/CRMWorkflow.swift), and a test holds it to this page and the reality file: only W1, W3, W5, W11, W12 and W13 are built, every workflow that changes records has a person cell, and no cloud model or agent cell runs in the app.

Explorers and villagers

A workflow can run as a vault app inside the CRM before it is built into the app, and a switch per section chooses which one runs: see vaults are the explorers, the app is the villagers.

What this does not claim

  • Only W1 exists, and it has not been run on a device.
  • The times are illustrative, for the team to correct, as on the Algarve Evolution page.
  • Cloud model and agent cells never run inside the app: the app holds no API key; they write proposals to a lane sealed to the user's key, and the app shows them for a person to accept.
  • The layer names (cell, workflow, play) are a proposal for AgentiCCells to take or change.
